# Chapter 13: The Authorities Circle

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

The authorities begin with questions.

A woman in a dark coat arrives at the washhouse with two constables behind her. She has a narrow face and a paper folder held against her chest. The brass buttons on her coat are polished bright enough to show the doorway in miniature.

“Oliver Wright,” she says.

“He is busy,” you answer.

“Doing what?”

“Making your visit inconvenient.”

Ada is beneath the workbench. Oliver stands beside the glider frame with one hand on the central spar.

The woman opens the folder. “This structure has been constructed on municipal property.”

“The washhouse is abandoned,” Oliver says.

“Abandoned property remains property.”

“Then it is very well cared for.”

One constable takes a step toward him.

You turn the chair sideways, blocking the narrowest part of the room.

The woman looks at you. “And you are Monica.”

It is not a question.

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

“You have been keeping records,” you say.

“We keep records of everyone.”

“Then you know my name.”

“We know your aunt's name. We know the landlady's name. We know that Oliver has entered the machine yard seven times.”

Oliver's fingers tighten on the spar.

Ada shifts beneath the bench. You hear the pencil in her sleeve knock against the floor.

The woman continues. “There have been complaints about theft, obstruction, trespass, and dangerous public demonstrations.”

“The demonstration lasted six seconds.”

“The danger lasted longer.”

“Did it injure anyone?”

“It could have.”

“So could the bridge.”

One constable glances toward the door.

The woman's eyes remain on you. “You are obstructing an official inspection.”

“I am sitting in a doorway.”

“Move.”

You turn the chair an inch. The door is still blocked.

“That is the most I can offer.”

Oliver says your name, low.

You do not look at him. “Do not interrupt your own defense.”

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

The woman opens the folder. Inside are drawings of the glider, copied from somewhere beyond the washhouse. The lines are poor, but the shape is unmistakable.

“This machine has no permit,” she says.

“Neither does the sky.”

“The sky is not a machine.”

“You have not inspected it.”

Ada makes a muffled sound beneath the bench. It might be a laugh. One constable bends toward the floor.

“There is someone else here,” he says.

“There are several people here,” you reply. “You have not counted the mice.”

The woman folds the drawings. “Oliver Wright, you are to come with us for questioning.”

“Am I under arrest?”

“You are being asked to cooperate.”

“That was not the question.”

The woman looks at him for the first time with something like respect, though it is not the kind he needs.

“Refusal will be recorded.”

“Then record it.”

The constable reaches for his shoulder.

Ada kicks the underside of the bench. A tin cup rolls across the floor and strikes the constable's boot.

Everyone looks down.

## [BEAT 4 — plea]

Ada comes out holding the map.

“She sent for me,” Ada says.

“Who did?” Oliver asks.

“The lady with the buttons.”

“I did not,” the woman says.

Ada points to the folder. “You have my drawing.”

The woman looks at the map. “You made this?”

“Some of it.”

“You are trespassing too.”

“I have been told.”

“How old are you?”

“Old enough to know when a road is false.”

The constable grabs for her. Ada steps behind the frame. Oliver shifts to cover her.

You seize the only opening left.

“If you take them,” you say, “you take the machine as evidence. If you take the machine, you must list every piece. If one piece disappears, the record will show whose hands were here.”

The woman turns toward you.

“You think paperwork protects you?”

“No. I think it makes people lazy.”

The woman studies the glider. She does not need to understand it to see what it means to Oliver.

[Stakes: real plea. Monica must keep Oliver and Ada out of custody. Success buys time and preserves the frame. Failure places Oliver at immediate risk of arrest and gives the authorities access to the workshop.]

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

“You are not a solicitor,” the woman says.

“Neither are you.”

“I represent the district.”

“Then represent its rules correctly.”

The paper folder opens again. “The district forbids unsupervised mechanical experiments by minors.”

“Oliver is not experimenting.”

“What is he doing?”

You look at the wing. “Repairing a damaged agricultural frame.”

Oliver's face turns toward you.

The woman notices. “Agricultural?”

“The wind turns it.”

Ada adds, “It could frighten crows.”

“That is not a permitted use.”

“Then write a new permit,” you say. “You are already holding a pen.”

“Agricultural,” the woman repeats, flat, as if setting the word down somewhere she can find it again. She does not believe it. Her eyes stay on the wing's shape a moment too long for that. But belief was never what she came here to collect — evidence was, and a crowd of witnesses at a doorway with no ordinance to cite is not evidence, only inconvenience.

The woman's mouth becomes a straight line. She looks at the two constables. Neither wants to make a choice that will require explaining the broken frame, the hidden girl, and a crippled witness with a clear voice.

At last she closes the folder.

“You have until tomorrow morning to remove the structure.”

“And if we do not?”

“Then it will be removed for you.”

She turns.

Oliver says, “You came for an arrest.”

“I came for compliance.”

“You found neither.”

“I found tomorrow morning,” she says. “That is not the same as losing.”

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

They leave. Their boots remain outside the door for a long time.

Ada puts the map on the bench. “Agricultural frame?”

“You are welcome to suggest a better lie.”

“A device for measuring clouds.”

Oliver looks at you. “That is not better.”

“It has a job.”

“So does the frame.”

The washhouse feels smaller. The glider fills the room. The door cannot open fully with the wing on its stand.

“We move tonight,” Oliver says.

“Where?”

“The old observatory.”

Ada shakes her head. “The road behind it is watched.”

“The quarry?”

“Too open.”

“The roof?”

“Too high for Monica.”

You tap the armrest. “I am here.”

They stop.

“Do not discuss routes as if I am luggage,” you say.

Oliver lowers his eyes.

Ada folds the map. “The printing office has a cellar. Two exits. One chimney large enough for the frame if we dismantle the skids.”

“Will they let us in?” Oliver asks.

Ada smiles without humor. “They owe me.”

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

The three of you begin packing.

Oliver removes the canvas. Ada labels each bundle with the red pencil. You keep the brass hinges in your lap and count them twice.

Outside, a horse stops in the road.

No one speaks.

Through the crack in the door, you see the brown coat. The Toll Man stands with the woman in the polished buttons. He points at the washhouse.

Oliver reaches for the frame.

“No,” you whisper.

“We have to go.”

“Not through the front.”

Ada lifts the map. “Cellar route.”

The horse moves on. The Toll Man remains.

You turn your chair toward the rear passage. The boards you made for the threshold are still there. Oliver carries the wing. Ada takes the tools. You carry the hinges.

At the first turn, the chair catches against a loose brick.

“Oliver,” you say.

He waits.

“Take the left side.”

He does not ask whether you can manage the rest.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

The rear lane opens behind the bakery. The city is dark except for the factory windows. People move in the gaps between lights.

Ada leads. She does not walk quickly. She walks exactly as fast as the wheels can go.

At the printing office, she knocks three times, pauses, then twice.

No one answers.

Oliver looks behind him.

“Again,” you say.

Ada knocks.

A hatch opens at ground level. An old man's eye appears.

“What do you want?”

“The cellar,” Ada says.

“No.”

You lean forward. “Sir, if you close that hatch, the authorities will find us. They may search your building. They may find whatever you are printing without a permit.”

The eye narrows.

“We will be quiet,” you add. “We will leave no mess. If we fail to keep either promise, you may tell us so in language suitable for a newspaper.”

The hatch opens wider.

“Bring the wing through,” the old man says.

Ada grins.

Behind you, a whistle sounds in the street.

The frame is still in the washhouse. The night has not finished deciding who it belongs to.
