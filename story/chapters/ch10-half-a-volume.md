# Chapter 10: Half a Volume

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

Oliver wakes you before sunrise by tapping the window with a feather.

You open it three inches. “If that is a bird, it has become very rude.”

“Dress warmly.”

“I am in a nightdress.”

“Then dress over it.”

He has packed bread, two apples, a tin cup, and the blue cloth from his coat. The glider is nowhere in sight. His face has healed into a yellow bruise.

“Where are we going?”

“Somewhere the sky is bigger.”

“The sky is the same size everywhere.”

“That is why we must test it.”

The landlady will object to the chair leaving the building. You tell Oliver to wait. You fetch your coat, retie the bandage, and use the crutch to stand long enough to reach the latch.

When you come downstairs, Oliver has bribed the landlady with an apple.

“That was my apple,” you say.

“It achieved its purpose.”

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

He takes you west through streets you have never seen. The chair handles rough pavement badly. Oliver slows whenever the wheels strike a seam, but he does not touch the back unless you ask.

Morning smoke lies low over the city. Laundry hangs between buildings. A butcher opens his shutters. Somewhere, a church bell counts a time nobody has agreed on.

“You chose the long way,” you say.

“The short way has stairs.”

“You remembered.”

“I remember most obstacles.”

“Only obstacles?”

“Also useful objects.”

He points to a discarded wheelbarrow, a broken sign, and a length of rope tied to a post.

“You have a remarkable mind.”

“It is crowded.”

At the edge of the city, the road becomes pale and hard. Grass appears between the paving stones. The air smells of salt though the sea is many miles away.

You stop at a rise where the land falls toward a wide plain.

Oliver removes the blue cloth from his coat and spreads it beneath your chair.

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

The cloth is not a blanket. It is a map.

The lines are drawn in charcoal and chalk. Roads loop around hills. Rivers are marked by threads stitched into the fabric. Some places have names. Others are blank.

“You made this?”

“I copied it.”

“From where?”

“A railway clerk's office.”

“Oliver.”

“The clerk left the door open.”

You look at the map. It shows the city as a dark knot. West of it, the plain stretches into empty cloth.

“You have never been here,” you say.

“No.”

“Then how do you know the land falls?”

“The birds do.”

He points to the horizon. Gulls move above the plain, their bodies bright in the morning light.

“They use the rising air,” he says. “See how they turn without losing height?”

You watch. The birds circle over something you cannot see.

“They are not proving anything,” you say.

“They are showing me where to look.”

## [BEAT 4 — dialogue]

He takes the apples from the basket and cuts one with a pocketknife. The blade is dull. Each slice comes out uneven.

You eat from his palm because the grass is too soft for the chair's wheels. He holds the pieces without comment. His fingers smell of metal and bread.

“What will you do when you fly?” you ask.

“Look.”

“At what?”

“Everything.”

“That is too much.”

“Then I will choose.”

He looks across the plain. The horizon is a clean line today. Above it, clouds gather in a long white shelf.

“Do you think there is anything above them?” he asks.

“Rain.”

“Above the rain.”

“More sky.”

“That is not an answer.”

“You dislike my practical answers.”

“They keep me from being comfortable.”

He offers you the last slice. You take it.

The sun warms the back of your neck. For once, nobody is asking either of you to move.

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

A shepherd finds you near midday. He approaches with a crooked staff and a dog that has lost one ear.

“Road is a mile back,” he says.

“We know,” Oliver replies.

“You lost?”

“No.”

The shepherd studies the map beneath your chair. “Making one?”

“Trying.”

The dog sniffs the glider's small brass hinge that Oliver has brought as a good-luck piece. Oliver lets it sniff.

“That hill ahead floods in winter,” the shepherd says. “The river cuts through the lower field. There is a stone bridge under the reeds.”

He points with the staff.

Oliver adds a mark to the map.

“Do you charge for information?” you ask.

The shepherd looks surprised. “No.”

“You should. People seem to charge for everything else.”

“Then pay me with the apple.”

Oliver gives him the apple.

The dog eats it in one bite.

“That was mine,” you say.

“The day is full of sacrifices.”

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

The shepherd leaves. Oliver lies on his back in the grass beside your chair.

“You are going to stain your coat,” you say.

“It has survived worse.”

He closes his eyes.

“Are you asleep?”

“No.”

“You look asleep.”

“I am practicing.”

The wind moves across the plain. It bends the grass in a broad, shining wave. Clouds gather and unmake themselves above the distant hills.

“What do you see?” he asks.

You follow the line of his gaze. “A cloud shaped like a boot.”

“That is what everyone sees.”

“Then a second cloud shaped like a badly cooked fish.”

“Better.”

“A third cloud shaped like a boy who should be repairing his machine.”

He opens one eye. “That cloud is rude.”

“It has conviction.”

For a while, you name clouds until neither of you remembers the names.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

On the way home, a wheel catches in a rut. The chair stops hard enough to throw your hands from the rims.

Oliver turns.

“Do not touch me,” you say.

He stays where he is. “Do you want the chair moved?”

“Yes.”

He lifts the front wheels over the rut. You push. The chair rolls free.

“Thank you.”

“You are welcome.”

The road slopes gently toward the city. Oliver walks beside you rather than ahead. The map is tied around his waist. One corner flaps against his hip.

“Today was good,” he says.

“The dog stole my apple.”

“That part was good.”

“The grass was damp.”

“Also good.”

“Your map is inaccurate.”

“It is unfinished.”

He looks at the line you drew beside the plain. It is crooked, but it marks the place where the land opened.

“You added that,” he says.

“I was there.”

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

At the edge of the city, the factory smoke hides the sunset. Oliver stops beneath a row of elms and takes the brass plate from his pocket.

He holds it against the light.

The hinges open. A narrow band of leather runs through them. It is the beginning of something that might fit around a head.

“Still a promise?” you ask.

“Still.”

“You are taking a long time.”

“Good work does.”

“Bad work also.”

“I am trying to avoid that.”

He puts the piece away before you can ask more.

At your building, he carries the map upstairs while you manage the steps by the rear passage. He goes slowly. Each time the chair catches, he waits.

In your room, he pins the blue cloth beside the window.

The city occupies only a corner of it.

The blank plain remains.

You sit beneath the map until the light is gone, and Oliver does not mention that half the world has no name yet.
