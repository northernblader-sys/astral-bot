# Chapter 20: Beyond the Astral

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

The second gust becomes a third.

The glider moves over the ridge grass. Its skids rattle against the stones. Oliver leans forward in the cradle, shoulder brace tight across his chest.

The woman in the dark coat shouts for the constables to seize the rope.

You wrap it around the chair's axle.

The chair jolts. Both wheels slide toward the drop. You brace your feet against the stone and pull the crutch from beside your knee.

“Monica,” Oliver calls.

“Fly.”

The left skid strikes a ridge stone. The frame lurches sideways. One constable reaches the rope and grabs it.

You shout, “Let go!”

He looks at you.

“That is not a suggestion.”

He releases the line. The second constable catches it instead.

The glider pulls hard enough to drag your chair forward.

Oliver reaches for the wing hinge.

## [BEAT 2 — plea]

The rope burns through your hands. The chair's front wheel leaves the ground. Beyond the stone wall, the ridge falls away.

The woman grabs the second constable's shoulder. “Cut the line.”

He draws a knife.

You speak before the blade reaches the rope.

“If you cut it, the glider turns.”

The constable hesitates.

“The wind is crosswise,” you say. “The frame will roll toward the crowd.”

The woman looks at the slope below. The authorities have gathered too close. A dozen people stand between the ridge and the plain.

“You are bluffing,” she says.

“I am reading the wind.”

Oliver shouts from the cradle, “She is right.”

You keep your eyes on the woman. “Let the rope go together. Clear the slope. Or cut it and explain why the first human flight became a falling machine aimed at your men.”

The woman studies you through the scratched lenses.

[Final obstacle: a fixed plea with the highest narrative stakes. Success makes the authorities release the line and clear the launch. Failure must still leave the glider a possible flight, but with a dangerous uncontrolled start and immediate danger to the crowd.]

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

The woman raises her hand.

“Release the rope,” she orders.

The constables obey. The line falls across the grass.

You pull it free from the chair's axle. The burn in your palms is bright and clean.

Oliver looks back at you. “Are you hurt?”

“You are about to leave the ground.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the one I have.”

The wind changes. The glider's canvas fills from the leading edge to the tail. The ribs bend in sequence, not like a bird flapping, but like a door opening into a room larger than itself.

Oliver lowers his head.

The skids race.

You push the chair backward. The front wheel lands. The rear wheel strikes the wall. Your body jolts hard enough to loosen the goggles.

The glider reaches the edge.

For one breath, Oliver is still on the ground.

Then the skids stop touching it.

## [BEAT 4 — battle]

The machine drops.

Oliver pulls the wing hinge. The central spar folds by one tooth. The glider noses downward toward the plain.

The crowd screams below.

You cannot reach him. You cannot call him back by strength. You can see the problem through the scratched lenses, a dark line where the right wing has begun to collapse.

“Oliver!” you shout. “Left brace!”

He turns his head.

“The shoulder brace. Pull the red strap.”

He yanks the strap. The brace releases. His body shifts left. The damaged wing rises just enough for the wind to catch it.

The glider drops another twenty feet.

The canvas tears along the seam.

Oliver reaches for the hand lever beside his knee. It is meant to open the second hinge. His fingers miss.

“Use your elbow!”

He strikes the lever. The wing opens.

The frame turns. The plain rushes sideways. The bridge flashes beneath him.

[Fixed encounter: The Last Fall. This is a spectacle-danger battle, not a fight against a person. The glider must recover from the uncontrolled drop so Oliver can complete the flight.]

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

The wing catches.

The glider rises ten feet, then twenty. Oliver's body disappears behind the central spar. The cap tied to it snaps in the wind.

You grip the stone wall.

Below, the authorities run along the ridge. The woman has forgotten her umbrella. It lies open and empty near the launch point.

The glider does not climb like a bird. It climbs like a thought someone has refused to abandon. Each gust gives it another piece of height. Oliver turns the frame toward the western hills.

He passes above the bridge.

The plain grows smaller.

A cloud shelf waits ahead. The underside is grey and bruised. Oliver's glider is a dark mark against it.

You remove the goggles. Without them, the distance loses its doubled edges. The machine becomes smaller.

You put them back on.

The lenses show two Olivers, one made of brass light and one made of shadow. Both rise.

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

The glider enters the cloud.

The crowd goes silent.

Oliver vanishes.

You count. One. Two. Three. The cloud swallows the wing. Four. Five.

The woman stands beside you. Her coat is wet through.

“He will fall,” she says.

You keep counting.

“The cloud is too thick.”

“You have never been inside one.”

“Neither have you.”

“That is why I am not giving an opinion.”

At twenty, a shadow appears above the cloud shelf.

It is not the glider.

The shadow grows wings.

Oliver rises through the top of the cloud.

The first human being to see above the clouds turns the machine in a wide, shaking circle. Sunlight strikes the canvas. The dark seams glow gold.

He is so high that the authorities become small figures on the ridge.

You can see him.

He can see everything.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

Oliver does not land until the sun has crossed the sky.

The glider comes down in the western plain, beyond the bridge, where the shepherd once said the ground flooded in winter. The canvas is torn. One skid is missing. The cradle has held.

By the time he reaches the ridge, he is walking badly and laughing at the same time.

He has something clenched in one hand.

“What?” you ask.

He kneels beside your chair.

“There is another sky.”

“I suspected.”

“The clouds have an upper side.”

“Did they?”

“They do.”

He opens his hand. A small white feather rests on his palm. It is not from a bird. It has broken free from the glider's patched canvas, its fibers lit with sunlight.

“You saw it,” you say.

“I saw the edge.”

He looks at the goggles. “Can you see through them?”

“Farther than before.”

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

The brass headpiece is finished before nightfall.

Oliver replaces the cloudy lenses with clear glass from the observatory telescope. He files the hinges smooth, adds a small wheel above the right temple, and fastens the leather without a single sharp edge.

When you turn the wheel, the lenses shift.

The bridge becomes clear.

The western hills draw nearer. A narrow road behind the observatory appears where the old maps left only blank cloth. Beyond it, roads you have never travelled begin to separate across the land.

The gamekeeper's daughter brings word from the town. Every gate will open to you now. No road will charge you. No known place will be closed. The places hidden behind distance, fog, and old maps are yours to reach.

You do not receive wings.

You receive the horizon.

Oliver sits beneath the window with the white feather between his fingers. Above him, birds cross the evening sky.

“Beyond that eye,” he says, “what can you actually see?”

You turn the brass wheel. The farthest road comes into focus.

“Enough to start,” you say.

The city below remains crowded, poor, and full of men who believe the world ends at the edge of their ledgers.

The sky has proved otherwise.
