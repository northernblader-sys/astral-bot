# Chapter 14: What It Costs

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

The printing office cellar smells of ink, coal, and wet stone. The old man gives you one lantern and no questions. Ada knows the room already. She leads Oliver through stacks of paper and presses covered with tarpaulin.

The glider has been reduced to bundles. The longest spar rests across Oliver's shoulders. Ada carries the map and a coil of rope. You take the brass box and steer through the narrow aisles.

“How far to the chimney?” you ask.

“Twenty-three paces,” Ada says.

“You counted?”

“I count everything.”

“Except potatoes,” Oliver says.

“Potatoes are temporary.”

Above, boots cross the printing floor.

The old man's voice rises. “No, officer, I have not seen them.”

A woman's voice answers. “We will inspect the premises.”

Ada turns the lantern down.

“There is another exit,” Oliver whispers.

“There is always another exit,” Ada says.

“Where?”

She points toward the wall.

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

The wall has a door painted the same black as the bricks. Ada finds the latch beneath a shelf of type blocks. It opens onto a service tunnel no wider than your chair.

Oliver goes first with the spar. Ada follows. You come last.

The chair's wheels scrape both walls. Mortar flakes onto your coat. Your left leg throbs beneath the bandage, trapped at an angle the narrow passage will not let you change.

“How far?” you ask.

“Forty paces,” Ada says.

“You counted this one too?”

“I count until I am safe.”

Behind you, the black door opens.

A constable shouts, “There!”

Oliver pushes forward. The spar catches against the ceiling.

“Lower it,” you say.

“I am trying.”

Ada turns around in the passage. There is no room for her to pass you.

“Monica, can you reverse?”

“Not quickly.”

“Quickly is not required.”

The constable's boots enter the tunnel.

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

You reverse the chair one hand at a time. The rear wheel catches on the wall. Oliver lowers the spar until it rests across Ada's shoulders.

“Give it to me,” she says.

“You cannot carry it and move.”

“Then I will carry it and not move.”

“Ada.”

“Go.”

She presses herself against the wall. The spar fills the tunnel between her and the constable. Oliver grabs the back of your chair and pulls.

The chair moves.

Your wheels scrape over stone. The pain in your leg becomes hot and sharp. You bite the inside of your cheek and keep your hands on the rims.

The constable reaches the bend. Ada raises the spar.

“Back,” she says.

The man stops. “Move aside.”

“There is no aside.”

“By order of the district.”

“The district will have to widen the tunnel.”

She glances at you. “Go.”

Oliver pulls harder.

## [BEAT 4 — battle]

The tunnel opens into a low chamber beneath the river embankment. Oliver drags you through first. Ada remains in the passage with the spar.

The constable lunges. The spar catches his chest and drives him against the wall. He strikes at the wood. Ada twists it aside, but the blow breaks the binding near the center.

Another constable enters behind him.

Oliver sets the spar down and runs back.

“No,” you say.

He does not hear you.

Ada kicks the loose type blocks from the shelf above the tunnel mouth. They tumble into the passage. The constables retreat as metal letters scatter across the floor.

The woman in the dark coat appears at the far end. “Ada Pike,” she says. “You are under arrest.”

Ada looks at Oliver. “Do not make that face.”

“What face?”

“The one that suggests heroism.”

She throws him the red pencil.

Then the ceiling beam gives way.

Dust fills the chamber. The broken spar drops between Ada and the exit. The second constable grabs Oliver by the collar as the first reaches for Ada's arm.

You drive your chair forward and shout until the woman stops them.

[Fixed encounter: The Net. The authorities close the route, and Ada's obstruction creates the opening for Oliver to escape. The encounter has a fixed loss. Ada does not survive the collapse.]

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

The dust tastes of chalk and ink. You cannot see the tunnel. You can hear Oliver coughing somewhere to your right.

“Ada!”

No answer.

The woman in the dark coat grips Oliver's collar. He claws at her hand.

“Let him go,” you say.

“Your friend has obstructed officers and endangered the public.”

“She is a child.”

“She is a repeat trespasser.”

“She is under the beam.”

“Then she should have moved.”

You turn your chair toward the woman. The chamber spins around the lantern light.

“You wanted a machine,” you say. “You wanted a thief. You wanted a charge to put in your folder. Take me instead.”

Oliver says, “No.”

“You have no permit,” the woman replies.

“I have a chair. I have a damaged leg. I have a name in your records. I will sit in your station and speak until every clerk knows exactly what happened here.”

The woman looks at the narrow chamber. Her face remains unreadable.

Behind the beam, Ada makes a small sound.

Oliver tears free.

## [BEAT 6 — battle]

He reaches the fallen spar. The wood has pinned Ada beneath the shoulder and chest. One of her hands is visible, fingers curled around the red pencil.

“Do not pull it,” she says.

“I can lift it.”

“You can break me.”

The constables seize him. He kicks, twists, and bites one man's wrist. The woman orders them to hold him.

You shout for the lantern. The light swings across the chamber. Ada's face is grey beneath the dust.

“Monica,” she says.

“I am here.”

“The river tunnel goes east. The second turn is flooded. Do not take it.”

“You can tell me later.”

“No, I cannot.”

The words are plain. She does not soften them.

Oliver goes still.

You put both hands on your chair's rims and push toward the beam. The front wheel strikes the wood. It does not move.

“Get help,” you say.

The woman glances at the blocked passage.

“You have men,” you tell her. “Use them.”

The constables lift the spar together. Ada's body shifts. The beam rises just enough for Oliver to crawl beneath it.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

Oliver drags Ada clear. Her eyes are open. The red pencil is broken in her hand.

He presses his coat beneath her head.

“Look at me,” he says.

Ada does. “Your face is dreadful.”

“You said that already.”

“I am repeating myself. It is a serious condition.”

You reach them. The chair cannot fit beneath the fallen beam. You stop at the edge of the chamber.

“She needs a doctor,” you say.

The woman kneels, two fingers at Ada's throat. She looks at the blood beneath Ada's coat.

“The nearest is across the river.”

“Then send someone.”

“The tunnel is unsafe.”

“Then carry her.”

Ada's hand finds Oliver's sleeve.

“Do not let them take the frame,” she whispers.

“Forget the frame.”

“You are terrible at following maps.”

Oliver bends over her.

“I marked the bridge,” she says. “Behind the observatory. It rises higher than the quarry.”

“Ada.”

“You promised.”

“I did not promise.”

“You should have.”

Her fingers loosen.

The lantern flame bends in the draft.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

Ada dies before the doctor arrives.

There is no final speech. No sudden return of strength. Her breath grows shallow while Oliver holds her hand and keeps asking questions she cannot answer. You watch the red pencil fall from her fingers and roll beneath the broken spar.

The woman in the dark coat stands over the three of you. The constables wait behind her.

“You cannot remain here,” she says.

You look at the beam, the crushed map, the blood darkening Oliver's coat.

“Move,” she says.

Oliver does not.

You put your hand on his wrist. His skin is cold.

“We have to go,” you tell him.

He looks at you as if the words are another language.

The doctor arrives too late. He kneels, checks Ada, and closes his bag.

The authorities take the broken frame. They leave the map where it lies.

Oliver carries Ada's cap.

You carry the brass box.

The tunnel out is narrow. Your chair scrapes the stone all the way to the river.
