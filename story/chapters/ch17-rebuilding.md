# Chapter 17: Rebuilding

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

Oliver brings the new frame to the washhouse in pieces carried under a tarp.

The first rib is blackened oak. The second is ash. The third has been carved from the straight leg of a school desk. They do not match. He has chosen them for what they can bear rather than how they look together.

“It is ugly,” you say.

“It is unfinished.”

“It was ugly before.”

“Now it has purpose.”

Ada's cap hangs from the nail above the bench. Oliver has stitched the broken red pencil into its band. The cap's brim points toward the west.

The new central spar is thicker than the last. It takes two hands to lift. The cradle is deeper, with a second belt across the hips and a brace for the shoulders.

“You made it safer,” you say.

“I made it harder to break.”

“That is not the same.”

“It is the part I can do.”

He sets the spar on the trestles.

The washhouse fills with the sound of a saw.

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

You keep the measurements while he cuts.

The slate is covered with old numbers. Ada's marks remain in the corners, red pencil pressed so hard into the surface that the wood has scarred.

“You are using her map,” you say.

“The map is gone.”

“Her marks are not.”

He follows the line of a previous measurement. “She was right about the wind.”

“She was right about most things.”

“Not all.”

“Give me an example.”

“She said the potatoes were temporary.”

“They were.”

“They lasted two days.”

“That is temporary.”

He begins to smile, then catches himself. The smile leaves a pale gap.

You look at the cap.

“You can laugh,” you tell him.

“I know.”

“You can remember her without making every room a grave.”

“I know.”

“You do not have to agree.”

“I know that too.”

The saw starts again.

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

The new wing has a hinge at its center. Oliver has built it from the brass plate in the box, cut into two sections and reinforced with teeth.

“That was for my head,” you say.

“A part of it.”

“You sacrificed it.”

“It was the right brass.”

“It was mine.”

“It is still yours.”

He fits the hinge into the spar. The teeth click together.

“You are stealing from my future device.”

“Borrowing.”

“The Toll Man says that word too.”

Oliver looks at the cap.

“Sorry,” you say.

“Do not be.”

He turns the hinge with a key. The frame folds inward, reducing its width by half.

“That is useful,” you admit.

“The old frame could not pass the river tunnel.”

“Neither could Ada.”

His hand stops on the key.

You place your palm on the workbench. “I said I was sorry.”

“You should not have to be.”

“Neither should you.”

He turns the key again.

## [BEAT 4 — dialogue]

The frame takes two weeks.

The city moves around it. Smoke darkens the sky. Rain swells the river. The Toll Man does not appear, though his absence is another kind of surveillance.

Oliver works until his fingertips split. You make him wrap them. He makes you hold the thread. Neither of you says the other's name when asking for help.

One morning, he brings the cap to the window.

“The bridge is beyond the observatory,” he says.

“Ada's road.”

“The rise behind it is high enough.”

“For what?”

“The launch.”

You look at the machine below. The new wing rests on its folding hinge. The canvas is pale brown, stitched with darker panels. It resembles no bird you have seen.

“When?”

“Soon.”

“That is not a date.”

“The wind does not keep a calendar.”

“The authorities do.”

“Then we go before they read it.”

He places the cap back on the nail.

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

Oliver asks you to test the cradle.

The seat has been built for his body, not yours. He removes the back brace and lowers the footrest until your injured leg can rest without pressure.

“You are not flying,” you say.

“I know.”

“Then why make it fit me?”

“Because the machine must fit the person who uses it.”

“It uses you.”

“It will carry me.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the answer I have.”

You sit in the cradle. The leather belts cross your chest. The frame surrounds you, all ribs and canvas. From inside, the washhouse roof disappears. The open doorway becomes a slice of sky.

Oliver checks the buckle.

“Do not tighten it.”

“It needs to hold.”

“I need to breathe.”

“Both can happen.”

He adjusts the strap.

For a second, the glider does not look like his machine. It looks like a room with no walls.

You grip the side rail.

“Get me out,” you say.

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

He frees you at once.

“You asked,” he says.

“You remembered.”

“I remember most obstacles.”

The phrase has become his.

You roll back from the cradle. The front wheel catches the new ramp. It holds.

“That part works,” you say.

“You sound surprised.”

“I am building a list.”

The list grows on the wall beside the charcoal measurements.

Ramp holds.

Hinge folds.

Belt does not choke.

Left wheel quiet.

Oliver adds one line beneath them.

Wind test pending.

You take the charcoal. “No.”

“What?”

“It is not pending. It is waiting.”

He looks at the words.

“The wind is waiting for you to stop being afraid of it,” you say.

“The wind does not know me.”

“It knows you are making it work.”

He rubs out pending and writes waiting.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

At night, the authorities come to the end of the road.

You see them from the window. Three lanterns. The woman in the dark coat. The Toll Man with his ledger.

Oliver is below, fastening the last canvas strip.

You tap the glass.

He looks up.

You point toward the road.

His face becomes still. He closes the washhouse door and turns the lamp lower.

The authorities do not enter. They stand in the rain for ten minutes, then leave.

“They know,” Oliver says.

“They have always known.”

“They will take the frame.”

“Not if we move it.”

“The bridge is watched.”

“Ada left another road.”

He looks toward the cap.

“Her road,” he says.

“Your map has not vanished just because the paper did.”

He touches the cap's brim.

“We leave before dawn.”

“No.”

He turns.

“We leave when the wind is right,” you say. “You are not allowed to trade one kind of foolishness for another.”

He nods.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

The new glider stands beneath the washhouse roof.

It is darker than the first. Its ribs are thick. Its seams show. Nothing about it suggests a miracle. It suggests work done after the miracle failed.

Oliver checks the shoulder brace. You check the ramp. The cap hangs above both of you.

“Do you think she would approve?” he asks.

“She would find three errors before breakfast.”

“Only three?”

“It is a new frame.”

He laughs. This time he lets it happen.

You take the brass box from the shelf. The headpiece inside is missing its main plate, but the leather band remains. Two curved pieces sit beside it, each filed smooth.

“Bring this,” you say.

“Why?”

“You said it was for seeing.”

“It is not finished.”

“Neither is the sky.”

He looks at the box, then at the wing.

Before dawn, the three square marks from Ada's map are copied onto the new spar.

One for the washhouse.

One for the chair.

One for the road worth returning to.
