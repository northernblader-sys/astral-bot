# Chapter 16: Why I Help

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

Oliver asks the question while tightening the chair's left bearing.

He has been coming every day. He brings the brass box. He brings no frame, no map, and no plans. He sits on the floor and repairs whatever is nearest to his hands.

“Why do you help me?” he asks.

The chair is tipped onto one side. You sit on the bed with your injured leg stretched out, crutch within reach.

“I do not.”

“You do.”

“I criticize you.”

“You do that as well.”

He turns the bearing with his thumb. “Why do you keep showing up?”

“I live here.”

“Why do you open the window?”

“It gets stuffy.”

“Why did you lie to Mrs. Vale?”

“The spoon was innocent.”

He looks at you.

You look at the ceiling.

“That is a question with too many parts,” you say.

“It has one answer.”

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

He sets the chair upright and tests the wheel. It turns silently.

“There,” he says.

“You have become very fond of that word.”

“It is useful.”

He parks the chair near the window. You lower yourself into it with the crutch. The movement is difficult after too much sitting. Your knee refuses to bend properly, and the floor feels farther away than it should.

Oliver watches the chair, not your leg.

“You can ask,” you say.

“I was not.”

“You were thinking.”

“I was thinking the bearing works.”

“Coward.”

He smiles faintly.

The brass box sits on the table. Ada's cap is inside. You know because Oliver has not repaired the torn crown, and the cloth shows through the hinge where the lid closes.

“Does it need a reason?” you ask.

“Everything needs a reason.”

“That is a very expensive belief.”

“It has cost me plenty.”

He touches the box.

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

You take the string he used to measure your head from the drawer. It has been knotted around itself.

“I help because you do not look away,” you say.

Oliver watches your fingers.

“From what?”

“From me.”

He frowns.

“People look at the chair,” you continue. “They look at the bandage. They look at the door behind me and decide how long it will take to get me through it. You look at the wheel. You look at the road. You look at the thing that needs doing.”

“I look at you.”

“Not first.”

He accepts the correction.

“You also never ask whether I want to be brave,” you say. “You ask whether I want the latch opened. You ask which route is level. You ask before moving the chair.”

Oliver sits on the edge of the bed.

“That is all?”

“No.”

“What else?”

“You make the window worth opening.”

He looks toward the alley.

Nothing moves below. The sky above the chimneys is the color of tin.

## [BEAT 4 — choice]

Oliver takes the string from your hand. “You could have let me go after Ada.”

Your answer can be:

- “You were not the only one who lost her.”
- “I was angry enough to keep you alive.”
- “If you leave, the window becomes a wall.”

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

He does not answer right away. Whatever you said, it stays with him a moment before he lets it go.

He folds the string into a small circle.

“You said you cannot fly,” he says.

“I said I cannot walk freely.”

“That is not the same.”

“It is close enough for people who want me to stay put.”

He turns the circle of string between his fingers.

“I can make you something.”

“You already have.”

“The headpiece is not finished.”

You take the brass box and set it on your knees. “You made this after we met.”

“I made parts of it.”

“For what?”

“For seeing.”

“I can see.”

“You can see what is in front of you.”

The words are careless. He knows it at once.

You close the box.

“And you can see what is above you?”

“Not yet.”

“Then perhaps neither of us should boast.”

His face tightens.

“I did not mean it that way.”

“You meant it some way.”

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

Oliver takes the box from you, but he does not open it.

“When I fly,” he says, “I can tell you what is there.”

“Birds.”

“You do not know that.”

“Clouds.”

“You do not know that either.”

“More sky.”

“You have said that.”

“It remains likely.”

He looks down at the brass lid. “Ada wanted the bridge.”

“She wanted to map the road beyond it.”

“I want to finish because she saw the route.”

“And because you want the sky.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He looks up.

“Do not turn her into an excuse,” you say. “Do not turn me into one either.”

“What should I turn you into?”

“A person who is here.”

The answer bothers him. You can see it settle somewhere he cannot reach.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

He returns to the chair's bearing. Not because it needs repair. He has already fixed it. His fingers move around the rim, searching for another task.

“I thought helping you would be the closest I came,” you say.

“Closest to what?”

“The edge.”

“Which edge?”

“The one everyone says is there.”

He waits.

“The edge of the road,” you say. “The edge of the room. The edge of what a body is allowed to do. I thought if I could stand beside someone going past it, the air might reach me.”

Oliver says nothing.

You rest the crutch against the chair.

“That is not noble,” you add.

“I did not ask for noble.”

“You asked for a reason.”

“I asked why you help me.”

“There it is.”

He nods slowly.

The room holds the answer without improving it.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

When Oliver leaves, he takes the brass box and the repaired chair bearing's old washer.

“What will you do with that?” you ask.

“Keep it.”

“It is worn out.”

“It carried you.”

“It carried a wheel.”

“That is where you were sitting.”

He puts the washer in his pocket.

Outside, the clouds have opened above the alley. A narrow bar of blue appears between the chimneys.

Oliver stops beneath it.

“Tomorrow,” he says, “I start again.”

“Start with the spar.”

“I will.”

“Then the cradle.”

“I know.”

“Then the parts for the thing on my head.”

He turns.

“You said you would not ask.”

“I have changed my mind.”

He smiles. It is not the old shield. It is smaller, but it stays.
