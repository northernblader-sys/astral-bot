# Chapter 12: Three Now

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

Ada returns with three pencils and a sack of potatoes.

“The pencils are for work,” she says. “The potatoes are for bargaining.”

Oliver takes the sack. “Who did you bargain with?”

“A woman who wanted the sack gone.”

“Why?”

“It was full of potatoes.”

You look at her.

“Some bargains are complex,” Ada says.

The washhouse changes once she is in it. Oliver keeps his tools on the left bench. Ada moves them to the right because she says left-handed people think better near a wall. Oliver is not left-handed. He moves them back. Ada moves them again.

You appoint yourself keeper of the pliers.

“A wise decision,” Ada says.

“A necessary one.”

“The difference is often discovered later.”

By noon, the three of you have eaten one potato raw and argued about whether steam counts as weather.

Oliver says no.

Ada says a cloud is only steam with ambition.

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

The new glider frame grows faster with Ada's hands. She cuts canvas while Oliver shapes the spars. You keep the measurements written on a slate.

“The left side is one finger longer,” you tell him.

“Which finger?”

“The ordinary one.”

“You have several.”

“You have two hands.”

Ada leans over the slate. “You should use a mark for the width of Monica's wheel.”

“Why?”

“The wheel leaves tracks.”

“The glider will not.”

“Not yet.”

She adds two curved lines to the corner of the slate. They resemble the wheels of your chair.

“That is not a measurement,” Oliver says.

“It is a reminder.”

“Of what?”

“That the machine has to pass through the door.”

Oliver looks at the frame. “It will.”

“You said that about the last one.”

“The last one passed through the door.”

“It also passed through the ground.”

You take the chalk from Ada before the argument can become an engineering principle.

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

Ada's map becomes a fourth member of the household.

It hangs beside the glider. It spreads across your lap when you sit beneath the window. Ada adds roads in red, rivers in blue, and places where a person may sleep without being moved along by a boot.

“What is this mark?” you ask.

She has drawn a star beside the old observatory.

“Warm roof.”

“Whose?”

“A widow's.”

“Will she let us stay?”

“She lets anyone stay who can repair a kettle.”

Oliver lifts a hand. “I can repair a kettle.”

“You can make a kettle leak in a new direction.”

“That is still direction.”

Ada points to a blank area beyond the western hills. “There is a tower there.”

“How do you know?”

“I saw its shadow.”

“A shadow is not a tower.”

“Then the tower is not a tower. We can sleep beside its shadow.”

You mark the place with a small circle.

Oliver watches you. “You are adding to it.”

“Someone has to make the map accurate.”

## [BEAT 4 — dialogue]

At dusk, Ada finds a loose wheel on the chair and tries to repair it with a spoon.

“Not that spoon,” you say.

She looks at the flattened metal beside your bowl. “This one has history.”

“It has a lie attached to it.”

Oliver stops tying a knot.

Ada studies the spoon. “What lie?”

“I said it was mine.”

“Was it?”

“No.”

“Then it is a small lie.”

“Small lies become large when people carry them.”

Ada holds the spoon out to Oliver. “Can you fix the wheel without this?”

“Yes.”

“Then the spoon is decorative.”

She sets it on the bench.

“I once told a magistrate I was twelve,” Ada says.

“How old were you?” Oliver asks.

“Eleven.”

“That is the opposite of a useful lie.”

“He wanted to send me to a home. The home wanted children who could scrub floors. Twelve sounded less breakable.”

No one speaks for a while.

Then Ada picks up the potato sack. “There are six left. No one is allowed to make a speech about them.”

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

The first time all three of you leave the washhouse together, the city seems to notice.

Oliver carries the folded wing. Ada carries the map. You carry the slate and steer your chair along the patched road behind the foundry.

People look at the three of you and decide on a story. Three thieves. Three orphans. A boy with a broken machine, a girl with a broken leg, and another girl who has made a coat from other people's mistakes.

At the bridge, Ada stops.

“We have gone too far,” she says.

“We are still in the city,” Oliver replies.

“That is what I mean.”

She points to a sign nailed to the wall. NO LOITERING. NO UNLICENSED CARTS. NO STREET CHILDREN.

“I am not a cart,” you say.

“They have not invented a word for us yet,” Ada says.

Oliver unfolds the wing. Wind runs through the cloth and lifts it like a sail.

The sign tears loose from one nail.

Ada watches it swing.

“That counts as a map correction,” she says.

## [BEAT 6 — choice]

A shopkeeper shouts from the bridge that you are blocking the road. Oliver begins to fold the wing. Ada's hand tightens around the map.

You decide how Monica answers:

- “We will move. Give us one minute and a little courtesy.”
- “If the road is yours, prove it.”
- “Charge us for the delay. We are collecting stories.”

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

The shopkeeper chooses not to argue. He turns back inside, muttering about children and trouble.

Ada watches the door close. “You make people go away.”

“Sometimes they make the mistake themselves.”

Oliver finishes folding the wing. The three of you continue along the river.

At a quiet stretch beneath the bridge, Ada takes off her cap. Her hair has been cut short with a knife. She pulls the red pencil from behind her ear.

“What is your mark?” she asks.

“My mark?”

“Every mapmaker has one.”

She draws a small square on the slate. Inside it, a single line points toward the corner.

“That means a place worth returning to,” she says.

Oliver looks at the washhouse keys hanging from his belt.

“Then draw one for the washhouse,” he says.

Ada draws the square.

You add a wheel beside it.

“That is not a mapmaker's mark,” she says.

“It is now.”

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

Rain starts before you reach home. Ada wraps the map beneath her coat. Oliver carries the wing above his head. You pull the chair through water that rises over the lowest spokes.

At the washhouse door, Oliver lowers the wing.

Ada looks at the three of you, dripping and covered in river mud.

“We look respectable,” she says.

“You look like a failed parade,” Oliver replies.

“A parade requires permission.”

Inside, you hang the map near the lantern. The blue cloth has begun to fray at the edges. Ada pins it carefully.

The three square marks sit together now. One for the washhouse. One for your chair. One for a road that does not appear on any official map.

Oliver lights the stove. Ada cuts the potatoes.

You hold the slate while they argue over the angle of the new wing.

The rain drums on the roof.

Nobody asks who belongs here.

They simply make room.
