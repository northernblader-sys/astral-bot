# Chapter 19: The Night Before

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

The launch waits because the wind changes its mind.

By dusk, the ridge is covered in fog. The authorities remain below the observatory with their carts. They cannot see the glider clearly, but they do not leave.

Oliver brings you back to the old keeper's room beneath the dome. A broken telescope lies across the floor. Its glass has been removed. The stone walls hold the day's cold.

You sit near the window with the unfinished goggles still around your head.

“They pinch,” you say.

Oliver kneels beside you and loosens the leather.

“Better?”

“Now they slip.”

He adds a strip of cloth beneath the band.

“You are making me look like a badly assembled insect.”

“You asked for sight.”

“I asked for comfort.”

“You asked for both.”

He adjusts the left hinge. The lens catches the lantern and throws a pale circle across the wall.

Outside, someone coughs below the ridge.

“They are waiting,” you say.

“So are we.”

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

Oliver has brought one loaf of bread. He cuts it with the bone-handled knife Ada gave him.

The slices are uneven.

“You are cutting it wrong,” you say.

“There is a correct way?”

“Ada would say there is.”

He holds the knife above the loaf. “What would she say?”

“That you have confused a meal with a map.”

“She said that about everything.”

“She would have found a new line for you.”

He cuts the bread. The crust breaks beneath the blade.

“She wanted to see the bridge,” he says.

“She saw it.”

“From the tunnel.”

“That still counts.”

“No.”

You take the slice from his hand. “You do not get to decide what counts for her.”

He leans back against the stone wall.

“You always do that,” he says.

“What?”

“Make the answer harder.”

“Easy answers have done enough damage.”

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

Night covers the ridge. The fog thins. Below, lanterns mark the authority line.

Oliver checks the glider every hour. He tests the belts, the hinge, the canvas, and the skids. You read the measurements aloud from the slate.

“The shoulder strap is secure.”

“The left spar is straight.”

“The hinge has one loose tooth.”

Oliver looks up.

“I am joking.”

“Do not joke about hinges.”

“You are becoming Ada.”

He turns back to the machine.

“That was not an insult,” you say.

“I know.”

On the wall, the old observatory's telescope mount points toward the ceiling. Dust has gathered in its gears. A small opening in the dome shows the stars.

Oliver follows your gaze.

“When I am up there, I might not see anything,” he says.

“You will see clouds.”

“What if they cover everything?”

“Then find their edge.”

“What if there is no edge?”

“Then you have found something.”

He nods as if you have given him an instruction for the machine.

## [BEAT 4 — choice]

Near midnight, Oliver sits beside your chair. He has not asked you to sleep. He has not admitted that he is afraid.

He says, “What should I hear from you before morning?”

You can tell him:

- “Come back with an answer worth arguing about.”
- “If you fall, I will still be here to insult you.”
- “Take Ada with you. Not the cap. Her whole impossible map.”

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

The answer settles between you.

Oliver reaches for the brass box. He opens it and removes the goggles' spare hinge.

“I did not finish them for you,” he says.

“I noticed.”

“I thought you would see what I saw.”

“That is a dangerous wish.”

“I know.”

He turns the hinge in the lantern light. “I thought if you could see the distance, it would be easier.”

“Easier to do what?”

“Stay.”

You touch the brass plate above your brow. The scratches in the lenses distort the room. The lantern doubles. Oliver's face appears at the edge of both images.

“I have stayed,” you say.

“Not because you had to.”

“No.”

“Not because you were trapped.”

You look toward the door, the steps beyond it, the road that leads down to the waiting authorities.

“Those things can both be true,” you say.

He folds the hinge into the box.

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

At one in the morning, Oliver asks whether you want to go home.

“Which home?”

“The room.”

“The window.”

“The chair.”

“You are naming furniture as if it is a family.”

“It is what I know.”

You look at the goggle lenses. Through them, the stars smear into faint lines.

“I am afraid,” you say.

Oliver's hands become still.

You have not said it before. Not to him. Not about the chair, the roads, the authorities, or the machine.

“Of him falling?” he asks.

“Of you returning.”

He waits.

“If you come back unchanged, I will have to find a way to be glad,” you say. “If you do not come back, I will have to keep the window open for someone who is not there.”

Oliver looks at the ridge.

“I do not know how to answer that.”

“Good.”

“Good?”

“It means you heard it.”

He rests his forehead against the chair's wooden handle.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

The fog lifts before dawn.

The plain appears beneath the ridge, silver with frost. The bridge is visible. Beyond it, the hills rise into a bank of cloud.

Oliver moves the glider to the launch point.

The authorities see him. Lanterns begin climbing the path.

He comes back to you and kneels.

“I need you to hold the rope until the second gust.”

“I know.”

“Do not pull too early.”

“I know.”

“Do not let go because they shout.”

“I know.”

“If something breaks, I will call.”

“I know.”

He looks at the goggles. “Can you see?”

“You are doubled.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Neither is the sky.”

He puts his hand on the arm of your chair.

“Thank you,” he says.

“Do not make it sound finished.”

He withdraws his hand.

The cap is tied to the central spar. The red pencil rests beneath the belt.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

The woman in the dark coat reaches the ridge.

“Oliver Wright,” she calls. “Stand away from the machine.”

Oliver climbs into the cradle.

The first gust strikes the canvas and twists the wing. The frame holds.

You take the rope.

The woman says, “Monica, do not do this.”

You look at her through the scratched lenses. Her face splits into two pale figures, each wearing the same polished buttons.

“You should have said that before the tunnel,” you reply.

The second gust comes from the east.

The glider shifts.

Oliver looks back.

You hold the rope.

The authorities shout. The plain waits below. The clouds hang above the hills, their lower edges bright with the first light.

Oliver raises one hand.

The rope pulls against your palms.

You do not let go.
