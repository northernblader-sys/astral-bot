# Chapter 11: A Second Set of Hands

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

Ada Pike steals the map from your wall on a Thursday afternoon.

You see her fingers through the open window, narrow and brown from coal dust. She lifts the blue cloth off its pin and folds it with care.

“Put that back,” you say.

The fingers vanish.

“I said put it back.”

Ada appears below the sill. She is about Oliver's age, perhaps a little older, with a cap pulled low over one eye. A red pencil is tucked behind her ear. She holds the map against her chest.

“You drew the river wrong,” she says.

“I did not draw the river.”

“Then whoever did is a liar.”

“You are trespassing.”

“You are keeping bad geography.”

Oliver comes through the washhouse door carrying a bundle of wire. He stops when he sees Ada.

“Give that back,” he says.

Ada looks at him. “You have built a wing without marking the wind direction.”

Oliver glances at the map.

“Give it back,” he repeats.

She folds it once more, perfectly.

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

Ada puts the map on the windowsill but keeps one hand over it.

“The hill west of the quarry is not a hill,” she says. “It is an old spoil bank. The ground shifts after rain. That is why your right skid dug in.”

Oliver's eyes sharpen. “You were there.”

“I have eyes.”

“You were there.”

“I collect information.”

“You collect coats, too?”

Ada looks down at her coat. It has three sleeves sewn into it, one of which is too short to reach her wrist.

“This one was abandoned.”

“By whom?”

“A gentleman who was careless enough to leave it unattended.”

You look between them.

“That is my line,” Oliver says.

“Then you have been using it badly.”

Ada slides the map toward you. “The bridge under the reeds is real. The shepherd was right. Your mark is not.”

“It is my mark,” you say.

“That is the trouble.”

Oliver sets down the wire. “What do you want?”

Ada points at the glider frame. “A look.”

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

Oliver blocks the washhouse entrance with his body.

Ada tilts her head. “If I wanted to tell the Toll Man, I would have done it.”

“You know him?”

“Everyone knows him.”

“You could be working for him.”

“I am not patient enough for ledgers.”

You point at the frame. “Let her see.”

Oliver looks at you.

“She knows something about the quarry,” you say.

“She stole the map.”

“She returned it.”

“After correcting it.”

“You have corrected worse things.”

Ada gives you a quick glance. It is the first time she has looked interested in you rather than the map.

Oliver steps aside.

Ada enters the washhouse and circles the glider. She does not touch the canvas. She studies the knots, the skids, the angle of the cradle.

“Your left spar is too proud,” she says.

“It is meant to hold the wing.”

“It is meant to hold the wind. Those are different jobs.”

Oliver crouches beside the joint.

Ada points. “There.”

He presses the wood. The spar shifts.

## [BEAT 4 — dialogue]

Ada has a joke for every broken thing.

The cracked bearing is called a philosopher because it refuses to move. The missing brace is called a widow because it has lost its partner. When Oliver tells her this is not how tools should be named, she says, “That is why tools have no poetry.”

She finds the right spar's weak point before he does. She sees a split in the grain beneath the binding and taps it with one finger.

“You need another length here.”

“I know.”

“You did not.”

“I was going to.”

“That is a future tense. The spar is breaking in the present.”

Oliver takes a knife from his pocket.

Ada catches his wrist. “Not that one.”

“Why?”

“It is dull.”

She produces a smaller blade with a bone handle. The metal is bright and narrow.

“Where did you get that?” he asks.

“A mapmaker's widow.”

“You stole it.”

“She gave it to me after I stole something else.”

You watch her place the blade in Oliver's hand.

“What did you steal?” you ask.

Ada smiles. “A map.”

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

She stays until dark.

At first, Oliver keeps asking her to move away from the bench. Then he begins asking for the thread. Ada holds the spars while he binds them. She knows how to tie a parcel so it will survive a river crossing. She knows how to fold cloth around a fragile instrument. She knows which knots tighten when wet.

“Who taught you?” you ask.

“A man who sold maps.”

“Where is he?”

“He sold himself a better life.”

“Did it fit?”

“Poorly.”

She marks the blue cloth with the red pencil. A new line runs from the city toward the coast.

“You cannot draw a road because you want one,” Oliver says.

“You cannot fly because you want to.”

He looks at her.

“That was not an insult,” Ada says. “It was companionship.”

“Your companionship is unpleasant.”

“It is accurate.”

She adds a small circle beyond the blank plain.

“What is there?” you ask.

“If I knew, it would not be blank.”

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

Ada eats the heel of the bread Oliver brought. She bites it with the concentration of someone solving a difficult problem.

“You can have the middle,” you say.

“The middle is where people hide the good part.”

“It is also softer.”

“Soft things make poor evidence.”

She offers you the heel.

“I have teeth,” you say.

“That is not a map.”

Oliver laughs from the floor. It is the first time you have heard him laugh without needing to use it as a shield.

Ada points at him. “There. That is useful.”

“What is?”

“A laugh that arrives before the danger.”

“You talk as if danger has a schedule.”

“It does. I keep a map.”

She pulls a folded paper from her sleeve. It is covered in tiny marks.

“What are those?” you ask.

“Places I have slept.”

“Why some are crossed out?”

“They were bad places.”

“And the stars?”

“Places where someone gave me food.”

The paper has more crosses than stars.

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

When the glider is covered, Ada asks for the route to the roof.

“There is no route,” Oliver says.

“There is always a route.”

“There is a ladder.”

“Then that is the route.”

She climbs before either of you can object. Oliver follows with the lantern. You remain below, listening to their footsteps cross the boards overhead.

“Do not fall through the roof,” you call.

Ada answers, “I have already fallen through worse.”

“That is not reassuring.”

“It was not meant to be.”

A moment later, her face appears in the high window. The roofline opens above her. She looks over the city, then turns toward the west where the sky has become copper.

“You can see the river from here,” she says.

Oliver comes beside her.

“And the old observatory,” Ada adds. “There is a road behind it that does not appear on any city map.”

“How do you know?”

“Because I put it there.”

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

Ada leaves after sunset. She takes no food, no brass, and no part of the glider.

At the door, Oliver says, “Come back tomorrow.”

She pauses. “Was that an invitation?”

“It was a request.”

“Requests are invitations wearing a hat.”

“Come back.”

Ada looks at you. “Does she agree?”

“I have not hired you.”

“That was not the question.”

You look at the repaired spar, the map, and the empty stretch of floor where Ada stood.

“Bring a better pencil,” you say.

Ada smiles. “I have three.”

She disappears into the dark.

Oliver closes the door.

“Her name is Ada,” he says.

“I know.”

“She sleeps behind the printing office.”

“You know a great deal about her.”

“I asked.”

“And she answered?”

“Some of it.”

You pin the map back to the wall. The new line toward the coast looks uncertain, but it points somewhere.
