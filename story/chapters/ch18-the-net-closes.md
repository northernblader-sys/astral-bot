# Chapter 18: The Net Closes

## [BEAT 1 — dialogue]

The authorities arrive before the wind.

They block the road with two carts and a line of men in blue. The woman in the dark coat stands between them, dry beneath an umbrella. The Toll Man waits beside the first cart, ledger tucked under his arm.

Oliver has the glider half-folded in the washhouse doorway.

“We could use the river road,” he says.

Ada's route passes behind the observatory. The bridge rises beyond the line of carts.

“The river road is watched,” you say.

“The roof?”

“Too high for the frame to reach from inside.”

He looks at the narrow lane behind the bakery.

A constable steps into it.

“They have closed every route,” Ada would say.

You hear her voice with such precision that you turn toward the cap.

Oliver touches your shoulder. “Monica.”

“Do not.”

“What?”

“Do not say my name as if it will bring me back.”

## [BEAT 2 — dialogue]

The woman in the dark coat calls through the doorway.

“Oliver Wright. Surrender the structure and come forward.”

Oliver folds the wing another inch.

“You have no permit,” she says.

“You said it was agricultural.”

“You lied.”

“You believed me for almost a minute.”

The Toll Man opens his ledger. “The frame is subject to seizure.”

“You already seized the first one,” you say.

“This is a separate violation.”

“Then it is a separate frame.”

The woman turns her attention to you. “You are not helping.”

“That has never stopped me.”

“If you obstruct, you will be detained as well.”

Oliver's hand finds the rear of your chair.

“Do not threaten her,” he says.

“I am explaining consequences.”

“You explain them like you enjoy them.”

The woman raises one finger. A constable steps forward.

You move the chair into the doorway.

“There,” you say. “Now you have a practical obstruction.”

## [BEAT 3 — dialogue]

The constable stops less than a yard away. He is young, like the one from the washhouse, but not the same man. His club hangs at his belt. His eyes keep dropping to your leg.

“Move aside,” he says.

“I cannot.”

“You can.”

“I can move backward. The glider is behind me.”

He looks past you.

“Then move the glider.”

“It does not have wheels.”

Oliver says, “It has skids.”

“I was not asking you.”

The woman steps forward. “Monica, this is your final warning.”

“You have given me three.”

“This is the final one.”

“You should number them.”

The Toll Man says, “She is buying time.”

“Yes,” you reply. “That is what conversations are for.”

The constable reaches for the chair's arm.

You grip the rim and shout toward the street.

“Witnesses!”

Shutters open above the road. Faces appear. The shopkeeper at the bridge steps outside. The newspaper boy pauses with a bundle beneath his arm.

The constable withdraws his hand.

## [BEAT 4 — plea]

The woman comes close enough for you to see rain gathered on the brim of her hat.

“You think a crowd changes the law.”

“A crowd changes what can be denied.”

“Oliver is a danger to himself.”

“Then take him to a doctor, not a cell.”

“He has already caused injury.”

You look toward Ada's cap. It hangs from the spar behind Oliver.

“You did that,” you say.

The woman's face tightens. “Careful.”

“You sent men into a tunnel after children.”

“We responded to a disturbance.”

“You arrived before anyone called for help.”

The Toll Man steps forward. “Enough.”

You turn to him. “You brought the ledger. Read the page about Ada Pike.”

He says nothing.

“Read where it says you found her alive.”

The crowd has grown. The woman watches the faces at the windows.

You place both hands on the chair's rims. “Give us the road. The frame leaves the district tonight. No one is hurt. No one has to explain why your net caught a dead girl and still cannot hold a boy.”

[Stakes: hardest real plea. Success opens a narrow route to the observatory and delays the authorities. Failure means the district closes the launch site and the glider is seized before Oliver can attempt the flight.]

## [BEAT 5 — dialogue]

The woman looks at the Toll Man.

He shakes his head.

She looks at Oliver. His hands are on the folded wing. He is standing between the frame and the road with no weapon, no permit, and no reason for anyone to believe he can succeed.

“You are asking me to authorize an escape,” she says.

“I am asking you to stop pretending this is orderly.”

“The district has rules.”

“Rules did not save Ada.”

The crowd shifts. Someone whispers her name.

The woman hears it.

The newspaper boy says, “Everybody knows about the tunnel.”

The Toll Man turns on him. “Go home.”

“I am working.”

“Then work elsewhere.”

The woman closes her eyes for one second. When she opens them, she points to the lane beyond the bakery.

“The road is clear until the observatory,” she says. “After that, you are trespassing on private ground.”

“That is not permission.”

“It is not protection either.”

Oliver begins to unfold the wing.

The constable looks at his superior.

She says, “Let them pass.”

## [BEAT 6 — dialogue]

The glider moves through the lane. Oliver pushes from behind. You steer. The frame's skids scrape the walls, leaving pale marks in the soot.

The authorities follow at a distance.

The woman walks with them. The Toll Man remains beside the carts.

At the end of the lane, the observatory rises above the roofs. Its dome is cracked. Ivy covers the lower stones. Behind it, the ground climbs toward a bare ridge.

The road is not marked on any sign.

Ada's road.

Oliver stops at the foot of the rise.

“We made it,” he says.

“Not yet.”

The wind has turned. It comes hard from the east, pushing cloud over the ridge.

Oliver tests the wing. The canvas pulls against the frame.

“It is too strong,” you say.

“It is enough.”

“Those are different.”

“I know.”

The woman reaches the observatory gate. “You have no right to launch from here.”

“Neither did the birds.”

“The birds are not subject to district law.”

“They are lucky.”

## [BEAT 7 — dialogue]

At the ridge, the ground drops toward the western plain. The bridge lies below, a pale line through the reeds. Far beyond it, hills vanish beneath cloud.

You brace the chair behind a stone wall. Oliver sets the glider on its skids.

The authorities spread along the path. The woman raises her voice.

“Oliver, if you enter that machine, I will order you detained.”

“After?”

“Now.”

Two constables climb toward him.

You roll forward. The chair's front wheel strikes a root and stops. Pain tears through your leg as you lurch against the straps.

“Monica,” Oliver says.

“Keep working.”

The woman reaches the top. “Move away from him.”

You look at the open drop beyond the wing. “I am not near him.”

“You are involved.”

“Yes.”

The constables are ten paces away.

Oliver checks the shoulder brace. His fingers shake.

You take the brass box from your lap and hold it out.

“Finish the promise,” you say.

He sees the unfinished headpiece in your hand.

For the first time all day, he breathes.

## [BEAT 8 — dialogue]

He fits the leather band around your head.

The brass pieces rest above your temples. They are unfinished, but the hinges hold. A narrow plate crosses the brow. Tiny lenses sit at either side, cloudy with scratches.

“You cannot see through those,” Oliver says.

“Then do not make me wear them.”

“The glass is not clear.”

“Neither is the weather.”

He adjusts the band until it rests without pressing your skin.

The authorities reach the ridge.

“Oliver,” the woman says.

He climbs into the cradle.

You grip the launch rope.

The wind pulls the canvas toward the clouds.

Ada's cap snaps against the spar.

Oliver looks at you. “If it tilts left…”

“I let go.”

“If it tilts right…”

“I let go.”

“If it rises?”

You tighten the rope.

“I hold on.”
