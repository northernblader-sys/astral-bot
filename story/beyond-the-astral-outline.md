# BEYOND THE ASTRAL — Volume Outline
Tagline: Beyond that eye, what can you actually see? The injustice in
this world, or the sanity we think we all have?

Book One of the Astral saga (each future book = a different
character's volume, separate roster addition — this outline covers
Book One only).

Setting: Old Britain, pre-flight era. Nobody has ever flown. Oliver
Wright, an orphan, is building a way to do it anyway, out of scraps,
stolen leather, and a dream nobody asked him to have.

POV: The player is Monica. Seated, one leg bandaged, gets around with
a crutch or a wheeled chair. She isn't a hero in this story — she is
the reason Oliver survives long enough to become one. She loves his
dream because she cannot fly either, and some nights it is the only
thing either of them has.

Oliver and Monica's bond is written warm and tender, deliberately
ambiguous — never pinned down as strictly romantic or strictly
platonic. The reader feels what they feel.

Subplot: Oliver is quietly building Monica something of her own
across the volume — a brass, wing-hinged goggle/headpiece (seen on
the Book One cover). Pays off as a special reward item at the end of
the volume alongside her character unlock, with its own effect (tied
thematically to sight/distance/discovery, given her final unlock is
about reaching places others haven't).

Beat types used: dialogue, choice (flavor, no mechanical branch),
plea (mixed — some scripted-outcome, some real stakes using fixed
scripted stats, never the player's own gear/level), and battle
(reserved — used rarely, only where physical danger is real).

Ending: Chapter 20. Oliver flies. Monica does not — and is not meant
to. Her reward is Chapter 20's unlock: free, feeless travel to every
location including hidden/undiscovered maps nothing else in the bot
reaches, plus the goggles as a companion item. She never gets wings.
She gets the whole world instead.

---

**Ch.1 — The Boy Who Watched Birds**
Beats: dialogue only.
Introduce Monica's daily reality — the window she sits at, the alley
below where a boy is always tinkering with something made of scrap
leather and bicycle spokes. First real exchange between them. Oliver
doesn't ask why she's always at that window. He just starts talking
to her like she isn't fragile, which is the first thing about him she
likes.

**Ch.2 — Nobody Owns the Sky**
Beats: dialogue, choice (what Monica says when he tells her his plan).
Oliver tells her the actual dream, plainly, for the first time: to be
the first person to see above the clouds. She's the first person he's
told. Choice affects only what she says back — belief, teasing worry,
or quiet honesty about wanting it too.

**Ch.3 — Scrap and Silence**
Beats: dialogue, plea (scripted outcome).
Oliver has no parents, no trade, no coin — he survives by scavenging
and small theft, and it's starting to catch up with him. First time
Monica has to talk someone down on his behalf (a shopkeeper, minor).
Scripted — she gets him out of it, but it costs her something small
(a lie she doesn't love telling).

**Ch.4 — The Ribs of a Wing**
Beats: dialogue.
The first real glider frame comes together in the workshop/alley.
Establish the mechanics of his obsession — how many failed frames
came before this one, what he's sacrificing to build it (meals,
sleep, safety). Quiet chapter. Hope, mostly.

**Ch.5 — Names We Don't Ask For**
Beats: dialogue, choice.
Backstory beat: what happened to Oliver's parents (kept implied/light
here, expanded later — don't spend it all at once). First time he
asks Monica anything about herself — her leg, her family, why she's
always alone at that window. Choice shapes how much she tells him.

**Ch.6 — The Toll Man**
Beats: dialogue, plea (real stakes, first roll).
First real external threat: a local enforcer/toll collector figure
who's noticed Oliver stealing materials. Monica pleads on his behalf
for the first time with genuine risk — failure here has a real
consequence (Oliver's frame gets confiscated or damaged, not him
hurt yet — save physical danger for later).

**Ch.7 — What the Chair Doesn't Stop**
Beats: dialogue.
Monica-centered chapter. Her injury, her limits, what she can and
can't do to help him directly. Establishes the ache underneath
everything: she cannot run to him if it goes wrong. She can only
ever be there in time if someone lets her.

**Ch.8 — The First Fall**
Beats: dialogue, battle (small, low-stakes — more spectacle/danger
than combat; e.g. the frame fails, he falls, something has to be
fought off or someone has to be pulled from wreckage under pressure).
The first glider attempt fails. Public, humiliating, physically
dangerous. This is the volume's first real taste that this dream
could kill him.

**Ch.9 — Laughed At**
Beats: dialogue, choice.
Aftermath of Ch.8. The town's reaction — mockery, or worse, the
authorities taking new interest in "the mad orphan boy" as a public
nuisance/hazard. Oliver's resolve cracks a little for the first time.
Monica's choice here is about how she rebuilds it.

**Ch.10 — Half a Volume**
Beats: dialogue.
Midpoint. Slower chapter — an actual good day. Oliver takes Monica
somewhere (even just to the rooftop, or has her wheeled somewhere
new) so she can see what he sees when he looks at the sky. This
chapter exists so the loss to come actually costs the reader
something real.

**Ch.11 — A Second Set of Hands**
Beats: dialogue.
Introduce a companion figure — someone who starts helping Oliver
build (this is where image 2's traveling girl fits, if you want her
as a real character: a fellow scrapper/street kid, sharp, funny,
resourceful, map-obsessed). She becomes important fast — to Oliver,
and to the story, precisely so her loss later will land.

**Ch.12 — Three Now**
Beats: dialogue, choice.
The three of them (Oliver, Monica, the new companion) functioning as
a found family for a stretch. Warmth chapter. Establish exactly how
much this new bond matters before it's threatened.

**Ch.13 — The Authorities Circle**
Beats: dialogue, plea (real stakes).
The world pushes back harder. Oliver's thefts/public glider attempts
have made him a marked figure. Real risk of arrest. Monica pleads,
this time with something actually on the line for the first time
since Ch.6 — a failure here has teeth.

**Ch.14 — What It Costs**
Beats: dialogue, battle (real, physical danger — this is the
chapter).
**THE LOSS.** The companion from Ch.11 is caught — protecting Oliver,
taking a risk meant for him, or pulled into danger while helping him
escape. This is scripted, fixed-stat, and it does not go the way a
game usually lets you save someone. Monica cannot save her. This is
the chapter the tagline is named for.

**Ch.15 — The Quiet After**
Beats: dialogue.
Grief chapter. No plea, no choice, no battle. Oliver wants to quit —
not because he's afraid of falling anymore, but because he isn't
sure the dream is worth what it costs other people. Monica has to
decide what to say to a boy who just lost someone because of her
inability to stop it either.

**Ch.16 — Why I Help**
Beats: dialogue, choice.
Oliver finally asks Monica outright — the "who are you, stranger, and
why do you help me" moment lands HERE, earned, not as an ending line.
Her answer (in whatever form the choice shapes) is the emotional
crux of the whole volume: she can't fly. She never will. She needs
someone to.

**Ch.17 — Rebuilding**
Beats: dialogue.
Oliver decides to finish it — for the companion he lost as much as
for himself now. The new frame is different: better, sadder, more
determined. Callback to Ch.4's ribs-of-a-wing imagery, deliberately
darker in tone this time.

**Ch.18 — The Net Closes**
Beats: dialogue, plea (real stakes, hardest roll in the volume).
Final act begins. The authorities move to stop the launch outright —
this is the last and biggest thing Monica has to talk/beg/plead her
way through, with everything on the line. This is the volume's
climax of tension, right before the climax of triumph.

**Ch.19 — The Night Before**
Beats: dialogue, choice.
Quiet the night before the real attempt. Oliver and Monica, honest
with each other for possibly the first time completely. Choice here
is about what she chooses to say to him, knowing tomorrow he might
fall again — or fly, and either way, something ends.

**Ch.20 — Beyond the Astral**
Beats: dialogue, battle-or-plea (final obstacle — physical or
authority-based, your call when we write it), then the flight itself.
Oliver flies. First human above the clouds. Monica watches from
below, as she always has — except now the game hands the player her
reward: unrestricted travel, hidden maps, no fees, ever again. She
never gets wings. She gets the sky's ceiling removed from her own
life instead.
